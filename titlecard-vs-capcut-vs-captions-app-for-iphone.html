Best Caption App for iPhone: Titlecard (Kapto) vs CapCut vs Captions vs Captiono

Updated November 2026 · By the Kapto developer

Short answer. Pick CapCut if you want a full video editor that also makes captions, and Captions if you want AI editing, avatars and dubbing. Pick Titlecard (listed on the App Store as Kapto) if you only want accurate word-by-word captions made on your iPhone, without uploading the video, for the lowest monthly price in this list. We make Titlecard, so every competitor fact below comes from its public US App Store listing, checked on 2026-11-09.

Step by step

  1. Decide whether you need an editorIf you also cut clips, add B-roll or effects, choose a full editor such as CapCut or Captions. If you only need subtitles on a finished clip, a focused caption app is simpler.
  2. Check where your video goesTitlecard recognizes the speech on the device and never uploads the video. For the other apps, read the privacy section of their App Store listing, because their listings do not say recognition is fully on-device.
  3. Compare the plan, not the download priceAll four apps are free to download. Compare the monthly and yearly in-app subscription prices in the table below, and check the App Store for current prices in your country.
  4. Try the caption style on your own clipIn Titlecard all 16 styles preview free on your own video before you pay. Do the same trial in any app you consider, so you judge accuracy and look on your own footage.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best caption app for iPhone?

It depends on the job. For a complete editor with captions, CapCut. For AI editing, avatars and dubbing, Captions. For only accurate word-by-word captions made on the phone at the lowest monthly price, Titlecard.

Is Titlecard the same as Kapto?

Yes. Titlecard and Kapto: Captions for Videos are the same app; the App Store listing may still show the name Kapto while the rename rolls out.

Which caption app is cheapest?

In the US App Store listings checked on 2026-11-09, Titlecard Pro is $5.99/week, $4.99/month or $49.99/year. CapCut lists Standard Monthly at $9.99 and Yearly at $89.99, Captions lists $9.99 monthly and $64.99 yearly, and Captiono lists $7.99 monthly and $47.99 yearly.

Does Titlecard upload my video?

No. Speech recognition and caption rendering run on your iPhone.

Does Titlecard edit video like CapCut?

No. It only adds captions: no cutting, effects, B-roll, translation or dubbing. Use CapCut or Captions if you need those.

Can I try Titlecard before paying?

Yes. Import, on-device transcription, editing the words and a live preview of all 16 styles are free. Saving the captioned video and exporting .srt need Pro.

Is there an Android version of Titlecard?

No. It is an iPhone app only; CapCut and Captions also run on Android.

Is there an app called Subtitled?

We could not find an app named exactly Subtitled in a US App Store search on 2026-11-09, so it is not compared here; Captiono is the closest dedicated subtitle app in the results.
